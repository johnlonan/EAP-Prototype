AI Signals

Every AI insight carries a ✦ sparkle, a confidence level (High or Moderate), and a plain-language rationale. They appear inline in the backlog, as panel cards in the insights view, and on the home feed. The signals are always recommendations — the human approves. This matters: AI in this prototype is a thought partner, not an automator.

"✦ Sprint 2 forecast: 62–80% completion range · Median 71% · High confidence · Monte Carlo over 3-sprint velocity history"
⬡

AI Is a System, Not a Feature

Each persona gets a completely different set of AI signals. James sees whether his acceptance criteria were signed off. Ananya sees which features are off-track. Priya sees which epics are misaligned to OKRs. Marcus sees a composite goal-health read. The ✦ sparkle is consistent everywhere — one voice, every layer of the hierarchy.

Switch from James → Ananya → Priya mid-demo and open the insights panel each time to show this.
📦

Signal Bundling

On Ananya's backlog there is a draft Feature generated from 22 separate signals: emails, Teams threads, and customer tickets that all pointed to the same problem. The system clusters them into one proposal rather than 22 noise items. The source signals are visible in the detail panel.

"✦ AI bundled 22 signals → draft Feature ready · Adaptive Biometric Re-enrolment · WSJF 12.4 — your decision to accept"
◑

The Confidence Gauge

The semicircle gauge at the top of the insights panel is the AI's context-aware health read. It changes completely per persona and per tab — it is not a vanity metric. Opening the panel is the first thing a PM or RTE should do; the gauge tells them whether the situation needs attention before they read a single signal card.

Ananya / Planning: "PI 26 confidence · 74% · trending down"  |  Dev / Kanban: "Sprint forecast · 64% · trending down"  |  Kenji: "Q3 budget committed · 82% · up"
⑂

Pull Request & CI Status on Stories

Open any story at Team level with James or Dev. The detail panel shows branch name, PR number and status (open, merged), CI result, test count, reviewer status, and deployment environment. This is the developer experience story: a dev sees everything they need without leaving the tool.

"feature/pin-fallback-handler · PR #1247 · Open · CI passing · 12/12 tests · 1 reviewer, awaiting approval · Deployed to staging"
☑

Definition of Done

The story detail panel shows a DOD checklist: Tests written, Code reviewed, Design reviewed, Docs updated, Demo recorded, No open bugs. The scrum master's insights panel also surfaces a readiness count ("2 of 8 items at DOD") — they see the team's readiness without opening every card.

Open "Handle fallback to PIN on failed biometric scan" (James persona, sprint board). It has a mixed DOD state and an open PR — both features visible at once.
◐

Analytics & the Financial Layer

Switch to Kenji to close the demo on the exec and finance view. The Analytics tab shows: a budget committed semicircle gauge (82%, trending up), capacity outlook per team, and AI signals about where velocity and budget are drifting apart. It shows the prototype reaches all the way from a developer's story to a CFO's budget read.

The AI signal here: "Teams 4 and 6 are at 60% capacity but consuming 78% of Q3 budget — sprint throughput suggests a reforecast is needed."
